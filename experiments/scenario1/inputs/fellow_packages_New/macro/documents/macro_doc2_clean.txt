Drivers of US interest rates
To decompose the shocks underlying changes in US interest
rates, this study utilizes a sign-restricted Bayesian vector
autoregressive (VAR) model with stochastic volatility, as in
Arteta, Kamin, and Ruch (2022). The model utilizes monthly
data spanning from January 1982 to December 2022 and
includes four key variables: 2-year bond yields; 10-year bond
yields; the S&P 500 index; and 5-year-ahead inﬂation
expectations. The 10-year yield is included in addition to the
2-year yield to account for the persistent portion of inﬂation
expectations and future monetary policy decisions.
Increases in US interest rates can be attributed to several
key drivers, including real activity developments (real shocks),
inﬂation developments (inﬂation shocks), and the monetary
policy reaction function (reaction shocks). Real shocks refer to
changes in interest rates caused by ﬂuctuations in US economic
activity. When an interest rate increase is due to real shocks, it
is accompanied by an increase in US yields, equity prices, and
inﬂation expectations. Inﬂation shocks reﬂect changes in
interest rates driven by shifts in inﬂation prospects. When an
interest rate increase is due to inﬂation shocks, it results in an
increase in US yields and an increase in inﬂation expectations,
but a decrease in equity prices. Finally, reaction shocks are
changes in interest rates driven by market perceptions of the
Federal Reserve's reaction function. When an interest rate
increase is due to reaction shocks, it results in an increase in US
yields and a decrease in equity prices, similar to inﬂation
shocks. However, unlike inﬂation shocks, reaction shocks are
assumed to lower inﬂation expectations.

Introduction
Increases in US interest rates in response to rising inﬂation can
negatively aﬀect ﬁnancial markets across the world and,
subsequently, economic activity in developing economies,
including in East Asia.
This Brief emphasizes the importance of comprehending
the dynamic characteristics of the factors that inﬂuence shifts
in US interest rates. With a more comprehensive understanding
of the underlying drivers behind these changes, policy makers
can make more informed decisions to face external
disturbances.
The Brief follows Arteta, Kamin and Ruch (2022) and
categorizes US interest rate changes as stemming from three
drivers: (1) real activity developments (real shocks), which
reﬂect changes in US interest rates that are prompted by
changing prospects for US economic activity; (2) inﬂation
developments (inﬂation shocks), which reﬂect changes in US
inﬂation expectations; and (3) the monetary policy reaction
function (reaction shocks), which reﬂect investors’ assessment
that the Federal Reserve's reaction function has changed
(become more hawkish or dovish).
The ﬁndings suggest that US interest rate hikes due to real
activity developments have relatively benign eﬀects on East
Asian economies. Real shocks are associated with higher
external demand and better equity and sovereign risk
outcomes, despite higher borrowing costs. However, US
interest rate hikes due to inﬂation developments and shocks to
the monetary policy reaction function adversely aﬀect
developing East Asian economies. These shocks lead to tighter
ﬁnancial conditions, which can reduce economic activity.
The results highlight the vulnerability of East Asian
economies to the recent US interest rates hiking cycles that has
been driven by a more hawkish Fed and higher inﬂation
expectations. These ﬁndings underscores the need for policy
makers in the region to monitor risks carefully and take
appropriate measures to mitigate any negative eﬀects on their
economies.
How do rising US interest rates aﬀect developing East Asian economies?

Daisuke Fukuzawa, Ergys Islamaj, and Franz Ulrich Ruch
This Brief examines the impact of US interest rate hikes on developing East Asian economies. The eﬀect will depend on the underlying
factors driving the changes in US interest rates. US interest rates can be decomposed as stemming from three drivers: (1) developments
in activity in the real economy; (2) inﬂation developments; and (3) reaction shocks, prompted by investors’ assessments that the Federal
Reserve has shifted toward a more hawkish or dovish stance. While rises in US interest rates driven by real activity developments have
relatively benign eﬀects on developing East Asian economies, those driven by inﬂation developments and reaction shocks adversely
impact developing East Asian economies. The Brief can help policy makers in East Asian economies better understand the risks associated
with the rises in US interest rates.

Increases in US interest rates driven by real activity
developments (real shocks) had a positive eﬀect on equity
prices throughout 2022. Similarly, rises in US interest rates
driven by real activity developments led to higher exports,
reﬂecting the increase in demand, and lower sovereign risk
spreads. They also appear to have had a negligible eﬀect on
reserves (ﬁgure 2).
Increases in US interest rates driven by inﬂation
developments (inﬂation shocks), however, depressed equity
prices in developing East Asian economies. Increases in US
interest rates driven by inﬂation developments are associated
with lower growth of goods exports due to lower external
demand and led to loss of foreign reserves in developing East
Asia economies.
Increases in US interest rates driven by monetary policy
reaction function (reaction shocks) led to a decrease in equity
prices, a decrease in real goods exports, loss of foreign
currency reserves, and higher sovereign risk spreads. Overall,
over the course of 2022, the net eﬀect on developing East Asian
economies of the diﬀerent factors driving US interest increases
was negative. For example, a 25-basis point increase in the
2-year US yields depressed equity prices by about 6 percent in
developing East Asia at the end of 2022 compared to the
previous year. While real activity developments in the United
States helped boost equity prices in the region, these gains
were overshadowed by declines driven from inﬂation
developments and the monetary policy reaction function in the
United States.

Spillovers to developing East Asian economies
Given the evolution of factors that explain US interest rates
movements, the impact of US monetary tightening on ﬁnancial
conditions and economic outcomes in developing East Asian
economies is likely to have also changed. To assess the impact
of these US interest rate shocks on ﬁnancial and economic

ahead on monthly data from as far back as May 1998 to
December 2022. Models include two lags and a dummy for the
global ﬁnancial crisis (January 2008–December 2009) and the

These adverse eﬀects are more likely when inﬂation
developments and monetary policy reaction are driving the

It is essential for policy makers to take appropriate
measures to mitigate any negative eﬀects of interest rate hikes
on the East Asian economies. They need to remain proactive in
identifying and managing the risks associated with such shocks.
Additionally, policy makers must develop and implement
policies that are robust enough to withstand the impact of
interest rate hikes. This can include measures such as
increasing foreign exchange reserves, implementing ﬂexible
exchange rate regimes, and introducing policies to enhance
ﬁnancial stability.
In conclusion, the vulnerability of East Asian economies to
US interest rate hikes driven by factors other than improving
demand is a cause for concern. Policy makers in the region
must remain vigilant and take proactive measures to mitigate
any adverse eﬀects on their economies. By doing so, they can
help safeguard the long-term economic growth and stability of
the region.
Increases in US interest rates during 2022 also hurt GDP
growth in developing East Asian economies. While real shocks
are associated with higher GDP growth, especially in Malaysia
and the Philippines, inﬂation shocks and especially monetary
policy reaction shocks depressed GDP growth. Increases in
interest rates due to monetary policy reaction shocks are
associated with a signiﬁcant decline in emerging market
investment and private consumption (Arteta, Kamin, and Ruch
2022). In contrast, real shocks lead to higher real exports. The
rise in US interest rates in 2022 depressed quarter-on quarter
GDP growth by 0.2 percentage points, on average (ﬁgure 3).
Policy relevance
The ﬁndings of this study demonstrate that developing East
Asian economies are susceptible to the impact of US interest
rate hikes. While policy makers in the region should remain
vigilant and closely monitor ﬁnancial tightening in advanced
economies, the response should be cautious and based on
rigorous analysis. When increases in US interest rates are
driven by improving demand, then nations in the region could
see some positive eﬀects on their economic activity.
